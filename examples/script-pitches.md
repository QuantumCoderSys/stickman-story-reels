# Five full Part 1 scripts in the SUNDOWN vein

Ready-to-build scripts that follow `references/viral-script-playbook.md`. Each has a logline, why it's fun, cast and voices, the HUD clock, the full line-by-line script with what's on screen, the vote, the clue, and how each branch starts. Use them as they are, or as models when writing your own.

**The shared formula:** one kid far from help (the distance as a number) · a threat with simple rules · a clock on screen that only moves one way · someone you love who makes it harder · a two-word physical vote with a cost on both sides · a rewatch clue after the vote.

| # | Story | Hook in one line | The clock | Vote | Build cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **THE WHEEL** ★ | Stuck at the top of a Ferris wheel. They can't climb, but the wheel lowers you one car every hour. | TO THE GROUND: 6 → 0 cars | CLIMB / STAY | medium (a wheel, rides) |
| 2 | **LAST CAR** ★ | The last train stopped in a tunnel. Something is tapping every window, car by car. You're in car 8. | DARK CARS: 1 → 7 | RUN / STAY | low (one interior, one side view) |
| 3 | **LOW TIDE** | A lighthouse on a rock. At low tide the road to shore appears, and so do they. The lamp just blew. | TIDE: 2.1 m → 0.0 m | FIX / CROSS | medium (sea, rock, beam) |
| 4 | **3:33** | Desert gas station rules. The car with no headlights pulls in, and your little brother is in the passenger seat. | 3:33 → 3:34 AM | GO / DARK | low (one store, one pump) |
| 5 | **ROW ONE** | Grandpa's scarecrow moves one row closer every night. Grandpa used to drag it back. He died four weeks ago. | ROWS LEFT: 1 · SUNSET countdown | DRAG / BURN | low (field, porch) |

## 1. THE WHEEL ★ (carnival)

**Logline:** You're thirteen, stuck in the top car of a Ferris wheel at a closed carnival. Below, pale eight-foot things walk out between the rides. They can't climb. But every hour, the wheel's backup motor lowers you one car closer to the ground.

**Why it's fun:** a giant visible clock (the wheel itself), plain maths the viewer can do ("five clunks, sunrise at 6:12"), and a vertical setting nobody else uses.

**Cast and voices:** narrator **Liam** · SAM, your best friend (no lines; he points and whispers in narration) · the park's recorded PA announcer = **George** with `fx: intercom`, cheerful and wrong.

**HUD:** `TIME 11:00 PM` (clock) · `TO THE GROUND 6` (drops on each clunk) · `SUNRISE 6:12 AM`

| # | Voice | Spoken | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Narrator, tense | It's eleven at night. You're at the very top of a Ferris wheel. And the carnival below you just closed. | **Frame 1:** the wheel against the night sky. Below, the rides' lights clack off one by one, rushing toward the camera. Your car rocks at the top. |
| 2 | PA (intercom) | The park is now closed. Thank you for visiting Brightwater Fair! | Speakers on poles. The last string of bulbs goes dark. |
| 3 | Narrator | You're thirteen. Your best friend Sam is next to you. Nobody comes to get you down. | Inside the car: you and Sam behind the safety bar. Below, the operator's booth is empty, its door swinging. |
| 4 | Narrator | Then the carousel lights turn back on. By themselves. | The carousel flickers on and the horses start rising and falling. |
| 5 | Narrator | Something walks out from behind it. | A thin pale figure steps out, eight feet tall. |
| 6 | Narrator | It's taller than the ticket booth. Its arms hang down to its knees. And it's looking up at you. | It stands beside the booth for scale, its head tilting slowly up. |
| 7 | Narrator | Then another one. And another. | Three, then five, gathering at the base of the wheel. |
| 8 | Narrator | Sam whispers that they can't climb. He's right. Their hands slide off the steel. | Long fingers grip a support, slip, and grip again. |
| 9 | Narrator | So they just stand there. Waiting. | Five pale faces turned up at you. |
| 10 | Narrator | Midnight. With a loud clunk, the wheel moves one car down. | **CLUNK:** the wheel rotates one slot and the camera jolts. HUD `TO THE GROUND 5`. |
| 11 | Narrator | The backup motor turns it one car every hour. | Inset: the motor box blinking, a timer at 60:00. |
| 12 | Narrator | Five more clunks, and your car is on the ground. That's five AM. | The car's path traced down the wheel to the waiting figures. |
| 13 | Narrator, quiet | Sunrise is at six-twelve. You'll be down there for over an hour. | HUD `SUNRISE 6:12 AM` flashes. (The contradiction, said out loud.) |
| 14 | Narrator | Sam points at the car above yours. It's empty. And its door is hanging open. | The top car, swinging, its gate open. |
| 15 | Narrator | Climb up, and you buy one more hour. But you'll be hanging forty meters up, in the dark. | Split frame, left: your hands on a cold spoke, the drop below. |
| 16 | Narrator | Stay, and you're safe in your seat. Until the wheel puts you down with them. | Split frame, right: the car sinking toward the pale faces. |
| 17 | Narrator, urgent | Follow, and comment your vote down below. Do you climb to the car above, or stay in your seat? Climb, or stay. The majority decides Part Two. | **CLIMB** / **STAY** |
| 18 | Narrator, whispers | And look at the operator's booth. The stop lever is pulled all the way down. Somebody stopped this ride on purpose. | Inset: the lever ringed. |

**Clue:** someone stopped the wheel with you at the top. Who wanted you up there?

**Where it goes:** **CLIMB:** halfway up the spoke, the carousel music starts, and they all turn toward it at once, as if someone's calling them. **STAY:** at 1 AM, a flashlight blinks from the funhouse roof in short-long-short: someone else is trapped here too. **Season:** the operator stopped the ride to keep you safe, because "they can't climb". He's hiding in the funhouse, and sunrise is the finish line.

## 2. LAST CAR ★ (subway)

**Logline:** You fell asleep on the last train home. It's stopped in a tunnel. Something is walking along the tracks, tapping on every window, and every car it taps goes dark. You're in the last car.

**Why it's fun:** the sound is the hook (tap, tap, tap), the counter only goes one way, and it's the cheapest one to build (one interior, one side view).

**Cast and voices:** narrator **Liam** · the DRIVER on the intercom = **Bill** with `fx: intercom` (calm, slow, a little too calm).

**HUD:** `TIME 1:47 AM` · `DARK CARS 0` (ticks 1 → 7) · `YOU: CAR 8`

| # | Voice | Spoken | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Narrator, tense | Tap. Tap. Tap. Something is knocking on the windows of your train. | **Frame 1:** a long pale knuckle taps a lit train window from the tunnel dark. On the third tap, that window goes black. **(It's the driver's cab: the clue.)** |
| 2 | Narrator | It's one forty-seven in the morning. You fell asleep on the last train home. | Wide shot inside your car: empty orange seats, a flickering strip light. |
| 3 | Narrator | Now the train is stopped in the middle of a tunnel. | Through the window: only tunnel wall and cables. |
| 4 | Narrator | You're sixteen. You're in the last car. And you're alone in it. | You (stick figure, green backpack) sit up, alone. HUD `YOU: CAR 8`. |
| 5 | DRIVER (intercom) | Passengers, stay in your seats. Don't open the doors. And don't look at the windows. | The intercom grille glows while he talks. |
| 6 | Narrator | Then you hear it again. Far up front. Tap. Tap. Tap. | Cut to the side view of the whole train, eight cars in the tunnel. |
| 7 | Narrator | Every car it taps goes dark. | Car 2 goes black. HUD `DARK CARS 2`. |
| 8 | Narrator | Car three. Car four. | Cars 3 and 4 go black, each with a tap. |
| 9 | Narrator | Through the door between the cars, you see its shape pass the window of car five. | Through the connecting door: a tall silhouette sliding past, bent to the glass. |
| 10 | Narrator | It walks bent over. Like it's looking in. | Close on the silhouette's head at the glass. |
| 11 | Narrator | Behind you is the back door of the train. Through its glass, far down the tunnel, you can see the last station. | The rear door, and a tiny lit platform far behind. |
| 12 | Narrator | Four hundred meters. In the dark. On the tracks. | Chip: `STATION · 400 M`. |
| 13 | Narrator | Car six goes dark. Then seven. The tapping is one car away. | HUD `DARK CARS 7`. The next car's lights die. |
| 14 | Narrator | Run, and you might reach the station. But the driver said never open the doors. | Split frame, left: the rear door handle, the long tunnel. |
| 15 | Narrator | Stay, and you keep your eyes down, like he said. And let it tap on your window. | Split frame, right: you, head down, a pale knuckle touching your glass. |
| 16 | Narrator, urgent | Follow, and comment your vote down below. Do you run for the station, or stay in your seat? Run, or stay. The majority decides Part Two. | **RUN** / **STAY** |
| 17 | Narrator, whispers | And look at the very first window it tapped. It was the driver's. So who's on the intercom? | Inset: frame 1 again. The dark window has DRIVER stencilled on the door. |

**Clue:** the driver's cab went dark first, before the intercom ever spoke.

**Where it goes:** **RUN:** halfway down the tunnel, the intercom comes back through the speakers on the tunnel wall: "Please return to your seat." It knows where you are. **STAY:** the tapping stops at your window. Silence. Then the intercom: "Good. Now look up." **Season:** each part is a station further down the line, every platform has a rule, and the voice on the intercom is something that learned to talk.

## 3. LOW TIDE (island lighthouse)

**Logline:** You live in a lighthouse on a rock two kilometers from shore. The stone road to the mainland only appears at low tide. So do they. Tonight is the lowest tide in eighteen years, and the lamp just blew.

**Why it's fun:** the tide meter falls the whole reel, the light beam is a moving safe zone, and the setting is beautiful to draw (moon, wet sand, the sweeping beam).

**Cast and voices:** narrator **Liam** · MUM on the marine radio from the mainland = **Alice** with `fx: radio`.

**HUD:** `TIDE 2.1 M` (counts down to 0.0) · `TIME 10:00 PM` · `SHORE 2 KM`

| # | Voice | Spoken | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Narrator, tense | Every night at low tide, the sea pulls back. And things walk out of it. | **Frame 1:** moonlit wet sand. Tall dark figures stand up out of the shallows, dripping. The lighthouse beam sweeps over them and they step back out of it. |
| 2 | Narrator | You're fifteen. You live in a lighthouse on a rock, two kilometers from shore. | Wide: the lighthouse on its rock, the mainland lights far away. |
| 3 | Narrator | When the tide goes out, a stone road appears. The causeway to the mainland. | The water drains away and the causeway surfaces. |
| 4 | Narrator | Grandad kept the lamp on every night. They never step into the beam. | The beam sweeps; a figure leans back out of it. |
| 5 | Narrator | Grandad's been in hospital for a week. Tonight, it's just you. | Grandad's empty chair and his cap on the hook. |
| 6 | MUM (radio) | Love, it's the lowest tide in eighteen years. Keep that lamp on. All night. | The radio's dial glows. |
| 7 | Narrator | Ten PM. The water drops lower than you've ever seen it. | HUD `TIDE` ticking down fast. |
| 8 | Narrator | And there are dozens of them. Standing on the sand. Watching the beam go round. | A crowd of silhouettes ringed around the rock, all heads turning with the beam. |
| 9 | Narrator | Then the lamp goes pop. And everything goes dark. | The bulb bursts. Black. Only their white eyes. |
| 10 | Narrator | In the dark, they start walking. | The eyes start moving toward the rock. |
| 11 | Narrator | The spare bulb is in Grandad's shed, at the bottom of the rock. Outside. | Looking down the spiral stairs to the shed door at the waterline. |
| 12 | MUM (radio) | Love? I can see the lamp's out from here. I'm getting in the car. | Across the water: a pair of headlights starts moving on the mainland toward the causeway. |
| 13 | Narrator | Mum is driving to the causeway. Straight toward them. | The headlights and the crowd, closing on the same road. |
| 14 | Narrator | Fix the lamp, and the light comes back for both of you. If you make it to the shed and back. | Split frame, left: the shed door at the bottom, figures on the sand. |
| 15 | Narrator | Cross the causeway, and you reach Mum before they do. In the dark, with them on both sides. | Split frame, right: the causeway lined with silhouettes, the headlights at the far end. |
| 16 | Narrator, urgent | Follow, and comment your vote down below. Do you fix the lamp, or cross to Mum? Fix, or cross. The majority decides Part Two. | **FIX** / **CROSS** |
| 17 | Narrator, whispers | And look at Grandad's shed. The door is already open. You never leave it open. | Inset: the shed door ajar in the first wide shot. |

**Clue:** the shed door was open before the lamp blew. Something is already inside the shed, or someone.

**Where it goes:** **FIX:** inside the shed, Grandad's notebook: "They aren't afraid of the light. They're afraid of what it shows." **CROSS:** halfway across, the headlights stop. Mum's door opens, and the figure that gets out is too tall to be Mum.

## 4. 3:33 (desert gas station)

**Logline:** You work the night shift at a gas station 200 km into the desert. The rules are taped to the register. At 3:33 a black car with no headlights pulls in. Tonight, your little brother is in the passenger seat.

**Why it's fun:** a rules device (viewers read along), one setting, and a twist that makes you check the frame again.

**Cast and voices:** narrator **Liam** · MOM on the phone = **Matilda** with `fx: phone` · LEO, your brother, 8, no lines: he only waves.

**HUD:** `TIME 3:31 AM` (clock, ticks) · `TOWN 200 KM`

| # | Voice | Spoken | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Narrator, tense | At three thirty-three every night, a car with no headlights pulls into your gas station. | **Frame 1:** the desert road at night. A black car glides in silently, no lights, and stops at pump 4. The clock flips 3:32 → 3:33. |
| 2 | Narrator | You're seventeen. It's your first week on the night shift. Two hundred kilometers from the nearest town. | The lit store in an ocean of dark desert. |
| 3 | Narrator | The last guy left you rules. Taped to the register. | A curled sheet under tape. |
| 4 | Narrator | One. Never go outside after three. Two. Don't look at the driver. Three. If it honks, turn off every light. | Each rule highlights as it's read. |
| 5 | Narrator | Every night, you follow the rules. Every night, it leaves at four. | Time-lapse: the clock to 4:00, the car pulling away. |
| 6 | Narrator | Tonight, the car pulls in. Then the passenger window rolls down. | The window lowering slowly. |
| 7 | Narrator | In the passenger seat is your little brother, Leo. In his pajamas. | Leo, eight, waving at you through the store glass. |
| 8 | Narrator | But Leo is at home. Asleep. Two hundred kilometers away. | Split: Leo waving here / a bunk bed at home. (The contradiction, said out loud.) |
| 9 | Narrator | Your phone buzzes. It's Mom. | The phone on the counter. |
| 10 | MOM (phone) | Is Leo with you? His bed is empty. | The waveform on your screen. |
| 11 | Narrator | Then the car honks. Three times. | The car's horn, the store lights flickering on each honk. |
| 12 | Narrator | Rule three. Turn off every light. | The rule ringed. |
| 13 | Narrator | Go outside, and you might pull Leo out of that car. But you break rule one. | Split frame, left: your hand on the door, the black car. |
| 14 | Narrator | Turn the lights off, and you follow the rules. But you lose sight of him in the dark. | Split frame, right: the store going dark, Leo's small hand on the glass. |
| 15 | Narrator, urgent | Follow, and comment your vote down below. Do you go outside, or turn off the lights? Go, or dark. The majority decides Part Two. | **GO** / **DARK** |
| 16 | Narrator, whispers | And look at the car in the store window's reflection. The passenger seat is empty. | Inset: the reflection, no Leo. |

**Clue:** in the reflection, there's nobody in the passenger seat.

**Where it goes:** **GO:** the passenger door is locked. Leo mouths "it's not me" and points at the trunk. **DARK:** in the dark, a small knock on the store glass at child height. Then the real Leo's voice from behind the counter: "Who are you talking to?"

## 5. ROW ONE (the scarecrow farm)

**Logline:** Every night, the scarecrow in Grandpa's cornfield moves one row closer to the house. Every Sunday, Grandpa dragged it back a hundred rows. Grandpa died four weeks ago. This morning, there's one row left.

**Why it's fun:** the opposite of SUNDOWN's speed. It's slow, patient and inevitable. There's a daylight clock to sunset, and a dilemma you can picture (drag it back or burn it next to the house).

**Cast and voices:** narrator **Liam** · GRANDMA = **Matilda** (dry, frightened, no filter).

**HUD:** `ROWS LEFT 1` · `SUNSET 7:48 PM` (counts down) · `NEIGHBORS 30 KM`

| # | Voice | Spoken | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Narrator, tense | Every night, the scarecrow in your grandpa's cornfield moves one row closer to the house. | **Frame 1:** a scarecrow between corn rows. Jump cut: one row closer. Jump cut: closer. The farmhouse at the end. |
| 2 | Narrator | You're twelve. You're spending the summer on Grandpa's farm. The nearest neighbor is thirty kilometers away. | A farmhouse alone in a sea of corn. |
| 3 | Narrator | Grandpa had one job every Sunday. He dragged the scarecrow back to the far end of the field. | Memory: Grandpa (stick figure, straw hat) hauling it over his shoulder. |
| 4 | Narrator | A hundred rows. Every single week. | The field from above, with a long arrow 100 rows long. |
| 5 | Narrator | Four weeks ago, Grandpa died. | The straw hat on an empty porch chair. |
| 6 | Narrator | Nobody's dragged it back since. | The scarecrow's tracks through the field, row after row. |
| 7 | Narrator | This morning, you count the rows. There's one left. | It stands at the last row, facing the porch. HUD `ROWS LEFT 1`. |
| 8 | Narrator | Up close, its arms aren't tied to the pole anymore. | Close-up: the loose rope; its sleeves hang free. |
| 9 | GRANDMA | Don't you touch it. Your grandpa never touched it after dark. | Grandma in the doorway, gripping the frame. |
| 10 | Narrator | In Grandpa's toolbox, you find his notebook. | The notebook and its last page. |
| 11 | Narrator | The last page says: if it reaches the porch, burn it. | The handwriting ringed. |
| 12 | Narrator | But the corn is dry. And the house is made of wood. | The dry corn rustling; the wooden porch steps. |
| 13 | Narrator | Sunset is in three hours. | HUD `SUNSET` ticking. |
| 14 | Narrator | Drag it back a hundred rows, like Grandpa did. But you'll be at the far end of the field when the sun goes down. | Split frame, left: you hauling it, tiny in the huge field, the sun low. |
| 15 | Narrator | Burn it right here, and it's gone for good. But you might burn down the farm with it. | Split frame, right: a match, the dry corn, the house behind. |
| 16 | Narrator, urgent | Follow, and comment your vote down below. Do you drag it back, or burn it? Drag, or burn. The majority decides Part Two. | **DRAG** / **BURN** |
| 17 | Narrator, whispers | And look at the first shot again. Every night, it faced the house. This morning, it's facing your window. | Inset: the scarecrow's sack face turned toward the upstairs window. |

**Clue:** it isn't heading for the house. It's heading for you.

**Where it goes:** **DRAG:** at row 60 it gets heavier with every step, and at sunset you feel the arms move on your shoulders. **BURN:** it doesn't burn. The flames go out the moment they touch the straw, and Grandma whispers, "He tried that too."

## Recommendation

- **THE WHEEL** has the strongest one-image hook and an original clock: the wheel itself, with maths viewers can do. It's the best AVD bet.
- **LAST CAR** is the fastest to build (two sets plus a side view), and the tapping is a ready-made audio hook. Its clue has the best "wait, what?" payoff.
- **LOW TIDE** is the most beautiful to look at. **3:33** reuses SUNDOWN's rules format. **ROW ONE** is the slow-burn counterweight to SUNDOWN's speed.

To build one: write its shots in `scenes.js` and its spec in `episode.json` (see SKILL.md, steps 3–7). The `creature()`, `kid()` and `stick()` helpers in `assets/kit.js` cover every cast here.
